The Gruffalo

A clever mouse invents a terrible monster to keep hungry animals away, until the monster is real.

The Gruffalo is a rhyming picture book by Julia Donaldson, illustrated by Axel Scheffler, first published in 1999. It is number 7 in our list of the best children’s books of all time. A clever mouse invents a terrible monster to keep hungry animals away, until the monster is real.

What is The Gruffalo about?

A mouse takes a walk through the deep dark wood. When a fox, an owl and a snake all think he looks tasty, he tells each of them about a terrible monster he is on his way to meet, a creature that does not exist. Then he meets one.

It was first published in 1999. The rhyme makes it easy to learn by heart.

Why children enjoy The Gruffalo

  • A rhyme that sticks in a child’s head.
  • A small hero who wins by being clever.
  • Great for joining in, with repeated lines.

Reading and talking tips

  • Let your child say the repeated lines with you.
  • Ask: how did the mouse trick each animal?
  • Make a story trail with props on a walk in the woods.

About Julia Donaldson

Julia Donaldson was born on 16 September 1948 in London. She studied drama and French at Bristol University, then worked as a teacher, performed songs as a travelling musician and became a songwriter for children’s television.

Frequently asked questions

What age is The Gruffalo for?

It suits ages 2 to 6. It is a popular first rhyming story for reading aloud.

Who wrote and illustrated The Gruffalo?

Julia Donaldson wrote it and Axel Scheffler illustrated it.

Where can I borrow The Gruffalo?
