Best books for ages 9–12

Gripping adventures, big questions and characters who feel real. Middle grade novels are where many children become readers for life.

Between nine and twelve, children are ready for longer stories, trickier themes and more complicated characters. They are also busier than ever, with school, friends, sport and screens. That makes this the age at which a gripping novel matters most, and the age at which many children quietly stop reading for pleasure.

The good news is that there has never been more choice. Fantasy, comedy, history, mystery and true-to-life stories all sit side by side on the middle grade shelf.

What to look for in a middle grade novel

  • A hook on the first page. A strong opening makes children want to keep going.
  • Characters who are a little older than the reader, so the book feels grown-up without being too old.
  • A plot that moves. Short chapters and cliffhangers help reluctant readers.
  • Themes that matter, such as friendship, fairness, family and courage.
  • A series or follow-up, so a child who loves a book can keep going.

Ten books for readers aged 9–12

Artemis Fowl

By Eoin Colfer

A twelve-year-old criminal mastermind tries to steal fairy gold, and the fairies fight back. Colfer, from Wexford, blends gadgets, humour and fantasy in a series that has charmed readers around the world.

Skulduggery Pleasant

By Derek Landy

A wise-cracking skeleton detective and a young girl called Stephanie investigate a magical crime. Landy is from Lusk, Dublin, and the series is fast, funny and spooky in just the right amounts.

Under the Hawthorn Tree

By Marita Conlon-McKenna

Three children set out across Ireland during the Famine to find their great-aunts. It has long been a favourite in Irish classrooms and a powerful way to introduce this part of history.

The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe

By C. S. Lewis

Four children step through a wardrobe into the land of Narnia. Lewis was born in Belfast, and his classic fantasy remains a go-to first epic.

Holes

By Louis Sachar

Stanley Yelnats is sent to a camp where boys dig holes all day, and there is a lot more going on than anyone admits. A clever mystery with several threads that come together beautifully.

Wonder

By R. J. Palacio

Auggie, who was born with a facial difference, starts school for the first time. A widely loved story about kindness and empathy, told from several points of view.

Harry Potter and the Philosopher’s Stone

By J. K. Rowling

An orphan discovers he is a wizard and starts at a school of magic. Its length is no barrier: the pace and humour keep readers going.

The Boy at the Back of the Class

By Onjali Q. Raúf

A group of children try to help a new classmate who has fled war. A warm, funny and hopeful story about refugees and friendship.

Percy Jackson and the Lightning Thief

By Rick Riordan

A boy discovers he is the son of a Greek god. Fast, witty and an easy gateway into mythology.

The Wordsmith

By Patricia Forde

In a future world where language is rationed, a young girl guards the old words. A thoughtful Irish dystopia for older readers in this age group.

Keeping tweens reading

Reading for pleasure often dips around age ten or eleven. A few things help:

  1. Let them choose. Autonomy matters more than ever at this age.
  2. Try audiobooks. Listening still counts as reading and often draws children into books they would not pick up.
  3. Follow their other interests. Football, space, animals and history all have great non-fiction and fiction.
  4. Read the same book. A shared book is a chance to talk, even if it is only for five minutes.
  5. Use the library. A librarian can often put the perfect book in a child’s hands. See our guide to children’s books and your local library.

Frequently asked questions

Are the Harry Potter books suitable for a nine-year-old?

Many nine-year-olds enjoy the first books in the series. They get darker as they go on, so some families read them together or pace them out. As ever, you know your child best.

What if my child wants to read books meant for older readers?

Check the themes first. Some books written for older teenagers deal with topics your child may not be ready for. A quick look at reviews or a chat with a librarian can help you decide.

How can I get my child to read more?

Make books easy to reach, keep a regular time for reading, and let your child choose what to read. Reading together for even five minutes can start a habit.

Are graphic novels okay for tweens?

Absolutely. They build vocabulary and visual literacy, and they are often the book a child finishes in a single sitting.
