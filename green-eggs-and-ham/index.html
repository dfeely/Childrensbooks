Green Eggs and Ham

A grumpy character is asked again and again to try a plate of green eggs and ham.

Green Eggs and Ham is a rhyming early reader by Dr. Seuss, first published in 1960. It is number 14 in our list of the best children’s books of all time. A grumpy character is asked again and again to try a plate of green eggs and ham.

What is Green Eggs and Ham about?

One character keeps asking another if he will try green eggs and ham. Here or there, in a house or with a mouse, the answer is always no, until he finally tastes them.

It was published in 1960 and is famous for using only 50 different words.

Why children enjoy Green Eggs and Ham

  • A tiny vocabulary that makes reading feel achievable.
  • A funny way to talk about fussy eating and trying new things.
  • Rhythm and repetition that help early readers.

Reading and talking tips

  • Take turns reading the lines.
  • Ask: what food did you think you hated and then liked?
  • Make something green and try it together.

About Dr. Seuss

Dr. Seuss was the pen name of Theodor Seuss Geisel, born on 2 March 1904 in Springfield, Massachusetts, who died on 24 September 1991. He wrote and illustrated dozens of children’s books.

Frequently asked questions

What age is Green Eggs and Ham for?

It suits ages 3 to 7 and is a popular first book for beginner readers.

How many different words does it use?

Only 50, which is part of its fame.

Where can I borrow Green Eggs and Ham?

Most public libraries stock it. See our guide to children’s books and your local library.
