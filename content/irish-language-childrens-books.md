Reading in Irish gives children a way into the language that school alone cannot always provide. A picture book in Irish at bedtime can make the language feel like part of the family, not just a subject.

You do not need to be fluent. Many parents learn alongside their children, and a book with clear pictures lets everyone work out the words together.

## Why read in Irish?

- **Vocabulary in context.** Children meet words in a story, not a list.
- **Hearing the rhythm.** Irish has its own music, and hearing it read aloud helps with pronunciation.
- **Confidence.** Reading in Irish for fun makes the language feel more natural.
- **Connection to culture.** Stories, songs and rhymes carry traditions with them.

## Where to find Irish-language books

Irish-language publishers produce picture books, early readers, novels and non-fiction. Look out for titles from **An Gúm**, the long-established state publisher of Irish-language books, and **Futa Fata**, a Gaeltacht-based publisher focused on children’s books. Other Irish-language and bilingual publishers also have children’s lists.

Many popular English-language picture books and novels have been translated into Irish, which is a great way to start: your child already knows the story and can focus on the language.

You can also:

- **Ask your library.** Many branches keep a shelf of Irish-language children’s books, and staff can order others.
- **Visit your school.** Gaelscoileanna and many primary schools have good collections and reading schemes in Irish.
- **Pair books with media.** TG4’s Cúla4 offers children’s programmes in Irish, which pair nicely with books on the same theme.

## If your own Irish is rusty

You do not need perfect pronunciation. Here are some tips:

1. **Preview the book.** Read it yourself first, or look for an audio version online.
2. **Lean on the pictures.** Point to what is happening and let your child supply meaning.
3. **Let your child lead.** Children who attend Irish-medium schools often know more than you expect.
4. **Learn together.** Look up the words you do not know and make a game of it.

## Frequently asked questions

:::faq
### Can I read Irish books to my child if I am not fluent?
Yes. Children benefit from hearing the language and seeing you take an interest. Start with simple picture books, use the pictures, and enjoy learning together.

### Where can I buy Irish-language children’s books?
Irish-language publishers sell directly, many Irish bookshops stock an Irish-language section, and your local library may also lend them.

### Are there bilingual books for Irish learners?
Yes. Some books present text in both Irish and English, which can be handy for learners, although many families find that children progress faster with books fully in Irish once they have the basics.

### What age should children start hearing stories in Irish?
Any age. Babies enjoy rhymes and songs in Irish, toddlers love simple picture books, and older children can move to chapter books and novels as their confidence grows.
:::
