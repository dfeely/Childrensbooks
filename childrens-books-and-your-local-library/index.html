Children’s books and your local library

Free books, friendly librarians and a story-time slot every week. Your local library is the most underused resource in children’s reading.

If you have not been to your local library recently, it is worth another look. Today’s libraries are bright, welcoming places with comfy corners, children’s events and a huge range of books, audiobooks and more.

Why the library is brilliant for children

  • It is free. Library membership is free in Ireland, so you can borrow as many books as your library allows without spending a cent.
  • Endless variety. Children can try new authors and formats without risk. If they do not like a book, back it goes.
  • Expert help. Librarians know children’s books inside out and love to recommend.
  • A regular routine. A weekly or fortnightly library trip becomes something to look forward to.
  • A sense of belonging. A child with their own library card feels grown up and part of a community.

How to join

Visit your local branch with identification and proof of address, as requirements can vary by council. Many libraries let children join as soon as they are born. Ask at the desk about your area’s rules, loan limits and how to get a card for a baby or toddler. You can find your local branch through the Libraries Ireland website.

What you can borrow

  • Picture books, early readers and novels, as well as non-fiction on any subject.
  • Graphic novels and comics.
  • Audiobooks.
  • Irish-language books, in many branches.
  • Ebooks and digital audiobooks, available in many areas through apps such as BorrowBox, with a library card.

Events to look out for

Libraries run all sorts of activities for children, including:

  • Story times and baby rhyme times, often weekly.
  • Author and illustrator visits.
  • Summer reading programmes. The national Summer Stars programme encourages children to keep reading over the school holidays.
  • Children’s Book Festival events, held in many libraries each year, usually in the autumn.
  • Homework clubs and craft sessions.

Check your library’s website or social media for what is on near you.

Tips for a great library visit

  1. Let your child choose. Give them a bag and let them pick, even if some choices surprise you.
  2. Borrow a mix. Some books to read together, some your child can read alone, and one just for fun.
  3. Try books you would not buy. The library is the perfect place to experiment.
  4. Ask for help. Librarians can suggest books by interest, age or series.
  5. Keep a library spot at home. A basket for borrowed books helps you find them on return day.
  6. Return on time. Teaching children to look after books and bring them back is part of the fun.

Frequently asked questions

Is library membership free for children in Ireland?

Yes, library membership is free in Ireland. Some libraries may charge small fees for certain services, such as overdue items or printing, so check with your branch.

Can I join the library with a baby?

Yes. Many libraries welcome babies as members, and baby rhyme times are a great way to meet other parents. Ask your local branch how to register.

Can I borrow ebooks and audiobooks for my child?

Many libraries offer digital lending through apps such as BorrowBox. You will need a library card, and availability may vary by council.

What if my child damages a library book?

It happens. Tell the library, who will explain what to do. A small amount of wear is normal and expected.
