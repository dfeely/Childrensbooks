There is a saying that every Irish family has a storyteller, and the country’s children’s books show it. Irish writers and illustrators have created picture books that are shared across the world, comic novels that have children giggling in classrooms, and historical tales that bring Irish history to life.

Below is a starting list. It includes authors from the island of Ireland, which means both the Republic and Northern Ireland, with their best-known titles and what to try first.

## Authors and illustrators to try

:::books
### Oliver Jeffers
*Picture books; Belfast*

Jeffers writes and illustrates quiet, funny and tender picture books. Try *Lost and Found*, the story of a boy and a penguin, *How to Catch a Star*, and *Here We Are*, a gentle welcome to the planet. He also illustrated *The Day the Crayons Quit*, written by Drew Daywalt.

### Chris Haughton
*Picture books; Ireland*

Haughton’s bold, colourful books are perfect for sharing with the youngest readers. *A Bit Lost* (also published as the board book *Little Owl Lost*) and *Shh! We Have a Plan* are great places to start.

### Eoin Colfer
*Fantasy and comedy; Wexford*

Best known for the *Artemis Fowl* series, in which a teenage criminal mastermind takes on the fairy world. For younger readers, try *The Legend of Spud Murphy*. Colfer was also Laureate na nÓg, Ireland’s children’s laureate.

### Derek Landy
*Fantasy; Lusk, Dublin*

His *Skulduggery Pleasant* series follows a wise-cracking skeleton detective and his young partner. Fast, funny and a hit with reluctant readers.

### Roddy Doyle
*Comic novels; Dublin*

Better known for adult fiction, Doyle has also written funny books for children, including *The Giggler Treatment* and *Rover Saves Christmas*, full of Dublin humour.

### Marita Conlon-McKenna
*Historical fiction; Dublin*

*Under the Hawthorn Tree* tells of three children surviving the Great Famine. It is widely read in Irish schools and is a moving introduction to history through a child’s eyes.

### Sam McBratney
*Picture books; Northern Ireland*

The author of *Guess How Much I Love You*, one of the most popular bedtime books in the world. McBratney writes with great simplicity and warmth.

### Martin Waddell
*Picture books; Northern Ireland*

Waddell wrote *Owl Babies* and *Can’t You Sleep, Little Bear?*, two perfect books about little ones needing reassurance. Both are ideal for bedtime.

### P. J. Lynch
*Illustration; Ireland*

A masterly illustrator whose detailed, painterly pictures bring stories to life, as in *The Christmas Miracle of Jonathan Toomey*, written by Susan Wojciechowski. Lynch has also served as Laureate na nÓg.

### Judi Curtin
*Chapter books; Ireland*

Her friendship-and-family stories, such as the *Alice and Megan* series, are popular with girls and boys moving into longer books.

### Patricia Forde
*Dystopian fiction; Ireland*

The author of *The Wordsmith*, a thoughtful novel set in a world where words are controlled. A strong pick for older readers.

### Oscar Wilde
*Fairy tales; Dublin*

Wilde’s tales, including *The Happy Prince* and *The Selfish Giant*, were published in the nineteenth century and are now in the public domain. They are beautiful, melancholy and worth sharing with older children.
:::

## Laureate na nÓg

Every few years, Ireland appoints a children’s laureate, called Laureate na nÓg, to promote reading and storytelling among young people. The role was created in 2010, and it has been held by authors and illustrators who travel the country, visit schools and champion children’s books. You can find out more on the [Laureate na nÓg website](https://childrenslaureate.ie).

## Helping Irish children find Irish stories

Children’s Books Ireland is the national organisation for children’s books, and its website lists new releases, award winners and events. Visit [Children’s Books Ireland](https://childrensbooksireland.ie) to see what’s new. Local libraries also take part in the annual Children’s Book Festival, usually in the autumn, with author visits and workshops.

## Frequently asked questions

:::faq
### Who is the most famous Irish children’s author?
That depends on the age group. Eoin Colfer and Derek Landy are among the most widely read Irish novelists for older children, while Oliver Jeffers and Chris Haughton are among the most celebrated Irish picture book creators.

### What are the best Irish picture books?
Good places to start are *Lost and Found* by Oliver Jeffers, *A Bit Lost* by Chris Haughton, *Guess How Much I Love You* by Sam McBratney and *Owl Babies* by Martin Waddell.

### Are there Irish children’s books about Irish history?
Yes. *Under the Hawthorn Tree* by Marita Conlon-McKenna is the best-known novel about the Famine for young readers, and many other authors have written about the 1916 Rising, the War of Independence and other periods.

### Where can I find Irish children’s books near me?
Your local library will stock many of them, and Irish independent bookshops are often happy to recommend titles by Irish authors and publishers.
:::
