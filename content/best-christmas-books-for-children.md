## Twelve popular Christmas books

Ages are approximate. Many of these work well beyond the age shown, especially when read aloud.

:::books
### Spot’s First Christmas
*By Eric Hill. Roughly ages 0–3.*

A lift-the-flap board book with the much-loved puppy hunting for presents and decorations. It is sturdy enough for little hands.

### The Snowman
*By Raymond Briggs. Roughly ages 3 and up.*

A wordless picture book about a boy whose snowman comes to life for one magical night. Telling the story in your own words is half the fun, and the soft pencil pictures are lovely to linger over.

### Stick Man
*By Julia Donaldson, illustrated by Axel Scheffler. Roughly ages 3–6.*

A stick-family father is carried further and further from home, with a long list of near misses, until Santa comes to the rescue on Christmas Eve. Rhyming and full of adventure.

### Mog’s Christmas
*By Judith Kerr. Roughly ages 3–6.*

A family cat gets into mischief during Christmas preparations. Warm, funny and full of recognisable family chaos.

### Father Christmas
*By Raymond Briggs. Roughly ages 4–8.*

A grumpy, grumbling Father Christmas gets ready for the big night. Told in comic-strip panels, it is a very different take on the legend and children enjoy the jokes.

### The Jolly Christmas Postman
*By Janet and Allan Ahlberg. Roughly ages 4–8.*

The postman delivers Christmas cards and letters to fairy-tale characters, and every envelope in the book holds a tiny letter, card or game to pull out.

### How the Grinch Stole Christmas!
*By Dr. Seuss. Roughly ages 4–8.*

A grouch tries to steal Christmas from the people of Whoville and discovers that it comes anyway. The bouncy rhymes are brilliant read aloud.

### The Polar Express
*By Chris Van Allsburg. Roughly ages 5–9.*

A boy boards a mysterious train to the North Pole on Christmas Eve. The glowing, dreamlike pictures make this a book to settle down with in the evening.

### The Christmas Miracle of Jonathan Toomey
*By Susan Wojciechowski, illustrated by P. J. Lynch. Roughly ages 6 and up.*

A lonely woodcarver is asked by a widow and her young son to carve a nativity scene. A gentle, moving story with rich paintings by Irish illustrator P. J. Lynch.

### The Christmasaurus
*By Tom Fletcher, illustrated by Shane Devries. Roughly ages 6–10.*

A boy who loves dinosaurs gets an unexpected Christmas visitor. Silly, fast and funny, with plenty of pictures.

### The Christmas Pig
*By J. K. Rowling, illustrated by Jim Field. Roughly ages 7–11.*

When a boy’s beloved toy is lost on Christmas Eve, a substitute toy sets out on a journey to find it. An emotional adventure for children who love their soft toys.

### A Boy Called Christmas
*By Matt Haig, illustrated by Chris Mould. Roughly ages 8–12.*

An imaginative origin story for Father Christmas, packed with elves, trolls and a brave young boy. Good for confident readers.
:::

## Classic poems and tales

Some of the most famous Christmas stories are old enough to be in the public domain, which means many editions are cheap and easy to find, including second-hand.

- **The Night Before Christmas** by Clement C. Moore, first published in 1823. Dozens of illustrated editions exist, so choose the pictures that you like best.
- **A Christmas Carol** by Charles Dickens (1843). Look for abridged and illustrated versions for younger readers, or read the original aloud to older children over several nights.
- **The Nutcracker** by E. T. A. Hoffmann, the story behind the famous ballet. Many picture-book retellings are available.

## How to choose a Christmas book

1. **Pick for the child, not the calendar.** A chunky board book suits a baby however festive the cover. An older reader may prefer a chapter book to a picture book.
2. **Think about how you will use it.** Quiet, gentle stories are good for Christmas Eve. Loud, funny ones work well on a December afternoon.
3. **Look for stories that last.** The best Christmas books still feel fresh on the tenth reading.
4. **Check your library first.** Many libraries put out a Christmas display in late November. See our guide to [children’s books and your local library](/childrens-books-and-your-local-library/).
5. **Order early if you are buying.** Popular titles can sell out in December, and delivery times stretch near the holiday.

## Start a Christmas reading tradition

You do not need many books to start a tradition. Choose one story to read on the first of December, another for Christmas Eve, and keep the rest in a box that comes out every year. ## Frequently asked questions

:::faq
### What are the most popular Christmas books for children?
Long-standing favourites include *The Snowman*, *Stick Man*, *The Polar Express*, *How the Grinch Stole Christmas!* and *The Jolly Christmas Postman*. For older readers, *A Boy Called Christmas* and *The Christmasaurus* are popular choices.

### What is a good Christmas book for a baby or toddler?
Choose a sturdy board book with bold pictures and little text, such as *Spot’s First Christmas*. Once toddlers are ready for longer stories, rhyming picture books such as *Stick Man* are a good next step.

### What Christmas books are good for 8-year-olds?
Funny, fast-moving stories tend to work well, such as *The Christmasaurus*, *The Christmas Pig* or *A Boy Called Christmas*. Reading a chapter a night is a lovely December routine.

### When should we start reading Christmas books?
Whenever suits your family. Many people start on the first of December, and some start in late November to build excitement. Others keep a special pile for Christmas Eve only.
