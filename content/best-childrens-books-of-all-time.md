There is no official list of the best children’s books ever written, so this is ours. We chose books that have stayed in print and in family bookcases for decades, that suit reading aloud or reading alone, and that have shaped the books children read today. We have avoided sales claims, because figures vary between sources. Dates are first publication, and ages are our own suggestions.

For what children are buying right now, see our [Top 20 children’s books](/top-20-childrens-books/), which changes with the bestseller charts.

## The 20 classics

:::books
### 1. Harry Potter and the Philosopher’s Stone
*By J.K. Rowling, 1997. Ages 8 and up*

The first book about a boy who discovers he is a wizard. It is a gateway to long reading, and a good read-aloud for younger children. The US edition is titled Harry Potter and the Sorcerer’s Stone.

### 2. The Very Hungry Caterpillar
*By Eric Carle, 1969. Ages 0 to 4*

A caterpillar eats its way through the week with holes to poke through the pages. It teaches counting and days of the week and suits very young children.

### 3. Where the Wild Things Are
*By Maurice Sendak, 1963. Ages 3 to 7*

Max is sent to bed without supper and sails to an island of monsters. The book won the Caldecott Medal and shows that big feelings can be safe to explore.

### 4. Charlotte’s Web
*By E.B. White, illustrated by Garth Williams, 1952. Ages 6 to 10*

A pig called Wilbur is saved by a clever spider. It is about friendship, kindness and loss, and works well as a chapter-a-night read-aloud.

### 5. The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe
*By C.S. Lewis, 1950. Ages 7 to 11*

Four children step through a wardrobe into Narnia. It is the best-known entry in a seven-book fantasy series.

### 6. Matilda
*By Roald Dahl, illustrated by Quentin Blake, 1988. Ages 7 to 11*

A clever girl with unkind parents discovers a special power. It is funny and fierce, and children love that the adults are the ones who get it wrong.

### 7. The Gruffalo
*By Julia Donaldson, illustrated by Axel Scheffler, 1999. Ages 2 to 6*

A mouse invents a monster to keep predators away, then meets him. The rhyme makes it easy to learn by heart. Read more about [Julia Donaldson](/julia-donaldson/).

### 8. Goodnight Moon
*By Margaret Wise Brown, illustrated by Clement Hurd, 1947. Ages 0 to 3*

A little rabbit says goodnight to everything in the room. It has a lullaby rhythm, and it is on our list of [bedtime stories](/bedtime-stories-for-children/).

### 9. Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland
*By Lewis Carroll, illustrated by John Tenniel, 1865. Ages 7 and up*

Alice follows a white rabbit down a hole into a world of puzzles and wordplay. It is a classic of nonsense and imagination, and older children enjoy it most.

### 10. The Cat in the Hat
*By Dr. Seuss, 1957. Ages 3 to 7*

A mischievous cat turns a rainy day upside down. Its limited vocabulary and bouncing rhymes made it a landmark in early reading.

### 11. The Tale of Peter Rabbit
*By Beatrix Potter, 1902. Ages 2 to 6*

A naughty rabbit sneaks into Mr McGregor’s garden. It is short, with small pictures that reward close looking.

### 12. Winnie-the-Pooh
*By A.A. Milne, illustrated by E.H. Shepard, 1926. Ages 4 to 8*

Gentle adventures with a bear of very little brain and his friends in the Hundred Acre Wood. It is full of warmth and quiet humour.

### 13. The Hobbit
*By J.R.R. Tolkien, 1937. Ages 9 and up*

Bilbo Baggins is swept into a quest with dwarves and a dragon. It is a good first fantasy novel for confident readers.

### 14. Green Eggs and Ham
*By Dr. Seuss, 1960. Ages 3 to 7*

Sam keeps asking if a grumpy character will try green eggs and ham. It uses only a small set of words, so beginner readers can manage it, and it is a gentle story about trying new things.

### 15. Charlie and the Chocolate Factory
*By Roald Dahl, 1964. Ages 6 to 10*

Charlie wins a golden ticket to Willy Wonka’s factory. It is a fast, funny story about greed and good manners.

### 16. The Wind in the Willows
*By Kenneth Grahame, 1908. Ages 6 to 10*

Mole, Rat, Badger and Toad share riverbank adventures. The language is old-fashioned, so it is best read aloud.

### 17. Anne of Green Gables
*By L.M. Montgomery, 1908. Ages 9 and up*

An imaginative orphan girl arrives at a farm on Prince Edward Island. It is a warm, funny story of growing up.

### 18. The Tiger Who Came to Tea
*By Judith Kerr, 1968. Ages 2 to 5*

A tiger arrives at teatime and eats everything in the house. It is a cheerful story for the very young.

### 19. Guess How Much I Love You
*By Sam McBratney, illustrated by Anita Jeram, 1994. Ages 0 to 4*

Little Nutbrown Hare and Big Nutbrown Hare try to outdo each other in love. It is a gentle bedtime book.

### 20. Diary of a Wimpy Kid
*By Jeff Kinney, 2007. Ages 8 to 12*

An illustrated school diary that turned many reluctant readers into keen ones. See [Jeff Kinney](/jeff-kinney/) and the latest in our [Top 20 children’s books](/diary-of-a-wimpy-kid-fight-or-flight/).
:::

## How to use this list

- **Match the book to the age.** Picture books such as The Very Hungry Caterpillar and Goodnight Moon suit under-fives. Novels such as The Hobbit work best when children are ready for longer stories.
- **Read the long ones aloud.** Older classics such as Winnie-the-Pooh, The Wind in the Willows and Charlotte’s Web are often easier to enjoy when someone reads them to you. See our guide to [reading aloud](/how-to-read-aloud-to-children/).
- **Borrow before you buy.** Most of these are in public libraries. See our [library guide](/childrens-books-and-your-local-library/).

:::faq
### What is the best children’s book of all time?
There is no single answer, because it depends on the age of the child and what you value. Harry Potter, Charlotte’s Web and The Very Hungry Caterpillar are among the most often named.

### Which classics suit toddlers?
The Very Hungry Caterpillar, Goodnight Moon, Guess How Much I Love You and The Tiger Who Came to Tea are all good for the youngest children.

### Which classics suit older children?
Harry Potter, The Hobbit, The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe, Matilda and Anne of Green Gables suit confident readers from about 8 to 9 and up.

### How were these books chosen?
We looked for books that have stayed in print and popular for decades, and covered a mix of ages and styles. The list is our own judgement, not a sales ranking.
:::
